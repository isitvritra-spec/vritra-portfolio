Microsoft’s Web3 Strategy

Microsoft killed Azure Blockchain Service in 2021 but didn’t give up on blockchain. Instead, they built practical tools that actually work with existing enterprise systems.

Azure Confidential Ledger is their main blockchain product now. It’s like a database that nobody can tamper with — not even system admins. It uses special secure hardware to guarantee data integrity while keeping things fast. Microsoft uses this internally to track their own supply chain and found millions in hidden costs.

Nethereum is your main tool for Ethereum

Nethereum is the go-to library when you want to connect .NET apps to Ethereum or similar blockchains. Version 5.0 supports everything from .NET 6 to .NET Framework 4.5.1, plus mobile platforms and even Xbox.

Performance is solid — you can deploy 500 smart contracts in about 6 seconds if you set up HTTP connections properly. It handles all the major token standards (ERC-20, ERC-721, ERC-1155) and works with Layer 2 networks like Polygon and Optimism for cheaper transactions.

And also the library integrates well with Azure Active Directory and supports enterprise wallet management. If you’re building business apps, these features matter more than the flashy DeFi stuff you see in crypto news.

Stratis took an interesting approach — they rebuilt their entire blockchain to be Ethereum-compatible while keeping their .NET heritage. This gives you a blockchain built by .NET developers for .NET developers, but you can still use standard Ethereum tools.

Companies That Actually Solves Real Problems

GE Aviation

They built TRUEngine to track airplane parts for 60% of global airlines. Before blockchain, tracking a part’s history took months of manual work. Now it’s instant. They also created a marketplace for used parts that were previously worthless — pure profit from better data.

The system connects to regular databases (SQL Server, Cosmos DB) because blockchain doesn’t replace your existing data — it verifies it. Airlines like Delta and Southwest participate because everyone benefits from better part tracking.

JpMorgan

JPMorgan’s network connects 200+ banks globally for faster international payments. Microsoft’s own treasury department uses this system, cutting letter-of-credit processing from 3–5 weeks down to 3–5 days. That’s measurable ROI, not hype.

Estonia

Estonia digitized 99% of government services using blockchain for document integrity. Citizens file taxes in 5 minutes (versus 8 hours in the US). The system doesn’t store personal data on blockchain — it just proves documents haven’t been tampered with.

Platform choice affects everything

Ethereum ecosystem through Nethereum gives you the most options. Layer 2 solutions like Optimism and Polygon reduce costs by 60–90% while keeping Ethereum’s security. Code optimization can cut gas costs by 10–40% through simple changes like variable packing and avoiding unnecessary storage operations.

Polygon offers multiple scaling solutions: Polygon PoS for immediate deployment, zkEVM for maximum compatibility, and CDK for custom chains. Transactions confirm in under a second with minimal fees. Your Ethereum code works without changes.

Hyperledger Fabric targets enterprise networks that need privacy controls. It handles 3,500+ transactions per second and runs well on Kubernetes. Banks and supply chain companies prefer this for regulated applications.

R3 Corda specializes in financial services with built-in legal contracts and privacy features. Major banks use it for trade finance. It became the first blockchain approved for European financial regulations in 2024. However, it doesn’t have native .NET support — you’ll need Java interop or REST APIs.

Job Market Favors Developers

Major consulting firms (Deloitte, IBM, Accenture, PwC) hire blockchain developers for enterprise implementations. Technology companies like Microsoft and ConsenSys offer direct blockchain development roles.

Freelance opportunities pay $75-$150 per hour for experienced developers, $150-$300 for specialists, and up to $500 for top consultants. High-demand areas include enterprise implementation, security auditing, and system integration.

Career progression offers multiple paths — technical tracks lead from developer to architect roles, while business-focused paths progress through project management to strategic consulting. The combination of .NET skills with blockchain knowledge creates premium market positioning.
