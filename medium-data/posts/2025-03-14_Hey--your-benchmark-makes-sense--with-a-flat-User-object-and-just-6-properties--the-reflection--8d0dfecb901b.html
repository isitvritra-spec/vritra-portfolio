Hey, your benchmark makes sense— with a flat User object and just 6 properties, the reflection overhead in .NET 8’s default System.Text.Json is already low, so source generation doesn’t show much gain (64.25 ms vs 64.96 ms). The article’s “10x boost” likely came from complex, nested models under heavy load (e.g., 1,000 concurrent users), not a simple List<User> with 100,000 items. Double-check your UserJsonSerializerContext has [JsonSerializable] and is partial; otherwise, you’re not fully testing source generation. For your case, the issue might be elsewhere—try adding nesting or concurrency to see the difference pop!
