### Saturday diary 📈

I was making $61,000 as a .NET developer and thought I was doing okay.

Then I met ‘Vivek Mahajan’ at a local meetup. Same experience level, same tech stack, same city. His salary? **$91,000.**

That $30K gap kept me up at night. What the hell was I doing wrong?

So I did something crazy — I started tracking the salaries of every .NET developer I could find. Built a spreadsheet. Asked uncomfortable questions. Dug into compensation data like my career depended on it.

> **Six months later, I was making $96,000. Eighteen months after that? $152,000.**

Here’s exactly what I learned from researching 200+ .NET developer salaries and coaching 47 people through negotiations…

## 🔹You lack Confidence, and that’s not my fault!

Most .NET developers are getting screwed. Not because they’re bad programmers, but because they suck at negotiations.

**Asked a Question in a Reddit forum and found that —**

- 67% had NEVER negotiated a salary increase
- 23% didn’t know their market value within $15,000
- Only 8% had any negotiation strategy

> top 10% of earners weren’t the best coders. They were the best negotiators.

## 🔹What .NET Developers Actually Make

After tracking salaries across major cities, here’s what I found —

> Skill level only explained 40% of the salary difference. The other 60% came down to company choice and negotiation skills.

## Five Things That Actually Matter

### 1. Know Your Numbers (Stop Guessing)

Most developers walk into negotiations blind. They guess their worth based on outdated Glassdoor data and this is **CAREER SUICIDE**

### Cheatcode

> — Built relationships with 5–6 tech recruiters (they know real market rates)

> — Joined private Slack channels where people share salary data

> — Maintained a spreadsheet with company, role, salary, and required skills

> — Checked in quarterly, not just when job hunting

> Recruiters will tell you salary ranges for jobs you’re not even applying for if you’ve built real relationships

---

### 2. Document Your Business Impact (Skills Don’t Matter)

everyone fucks up. They talk about technologies instead of business results.

**Template**

```makefile
Project: [What you built]
Problem: [Business issue you solved]  
Result: [Quantified impact]
Tech: [Tools you used]
```

---

### 3. Time It Right (Most People Are Too Late)

**The 90-Day Rule:** Start preparing 90 days before your review. Company budgets get locked 60–90 days early. Wait until review time? You’re screwed.

**Best timing windows:**

- 2–4 weeks after completing a high-impact project
- When you have competing offers (even if you won’t take them)
- January-March (budget planning season)

---

### 4. The Actual Negotiation (What Really Works)

Forget everything you’ve read about negotiation tactics. Here’s what actually works:

**Never give a single number.** Always a range where your target is the bottom 25%.

**Wrong:** “I want $100K”
 **Right:** “I’m seeing ranges of $95K to $115K for this impact level”

**Connect every request to business value:** “I’m requesting this because my payment system work generated $2.3M in revenue”

**Have alternatives ready:**

- 6-month salary review
- Additional PTO
- Learning budget
- Title bump with future raise

---

### 5. Build Your Exit Strategy (The Ultimate Leverage)

The best negotiation tool? The ability to walk away.

I always maintain relationships with 3–5 companies. Not being disloyal — it’s career insurance.

**My network maintenance:**

- 2 hours/month on LinkedIn engagement
- Quarterly coffee chats with former colleagues
- Technical blog posts (builds reputation)
- Local meetup participation

## Exact Scripts I Use

**Opening:** “I’d like to discuss my compensation based on the value I’ve delivered and current market conditions.”

**Presenting your case:** “I’ve documented my business impact. The [project] generated [value]. Market research shows [range] for this level.”

**Leveraging other offers:** “I’ve been approached by other companies, but wanted to discuss this with you first.”
