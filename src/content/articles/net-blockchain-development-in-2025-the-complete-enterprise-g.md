## Nethereum is your main tool for Ethereum

**Nethereum** is the go-to library when you want to connect .NET apps to Ethereum or similar blockchains. Version 5.0 supports everything from .NET 6 to .NET Framework 4.5.1, plus mobile platforms and even Xbox.

Performance is solid — you can deploy 500 smart contracts in about 6 seconds if you set up HTTP connections properly. It handles all the major token standards (ERC-20, ERC-721, ERC-1155) and works with Layer 2 networks like Polygon and Optimism for cheaper transactions.

And also the library integrates well with Azure Active Directory and supports enterprise wallet management. If you’re building business apps, these features matter more than the flashy DeFi stuff you see in crypto news.

> **Stratis** took an interesting approach — they rebuilt their entire blockchain to be Ethereum-compatible while keeping their .NET heritage. This gives you a blockchain built by .NET developers for .NET developers, but you can still use standard Ethereum tools.

## **Platform choice affects everything**

**Ethereum ecosystem** through Nethereum gives you the most options. Layer 2 solutions like Optimism and Polygon reduce costs by 60–90% while keeping Ethereum’s security. Code optimization can cut gas costs by 10–40% through simple changes like variable packing and avoiding unnecessary storage operations.

**Polygon** offers multiple scaling solutions: Polygon PoS for immediate deployment, zkEVM for maximum compatibility, and CDK for custom chains. Transactions confirm in under a second with minimal fees. Your Ethereum code works without changes.

**Hyperledger Fabric** targets enterprise networks that need privacy controls. It handles 3,500+ transactions per second and runs well on Kubernetes. Banks and supply chain companies prefer this for regulated applications.

**R3 Corda** specializes in financial services with built-in legal contracts and privacy features. Major banks use it for trade finance. It became the first blockchain approved for European financial regulations in 2024. However, it doesn’t have native .NET support — you’ll need Java interop or REST APIs.
